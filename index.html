01
Live Intelligence
Site Scout

The parcel belongs to the Weiter family. Carol Weiter, individual ownership, $0 last sale amount in the public record. That combination tells a story: family-held for a very long time, never professionally marketed, pricing set by emotion not market. Multiple price drops and an individual owner on the other side of the table. You're talking to a person, not a team.

The listing covers three addresses: 1267, 1269, and 1271 S. Shelby. The 1271 building covers 93% of its lot. 4,564 square feet of floor area on 4,922 square feet of land. But 1267 is a vacant buildable lot, confirmed by MK Lindsey on May 10, 2026. That changes everything. The combined site supports both adaptive reuse of the historic building AND new vertical construction. The program options expand materially beyond a single-building rehab.

Shelby Park became a National Register Historic District in 2024. But the building’s contributing status is the single most important open question. The Regulatory Scout found every neighbor on this block coded non-contributing. The team should assume NC until KHC says otherwise. MK Lindsey notes that each building is evaluated individually, not as a block. If contributing, historic tax credits open up. If not, the Allison Cleaners appeal pathway is on the table.

Red flags: Phase I ESA is mandatory on 135 years of industrial use. Building condition is unknown until site visit. Foundation, roof, structural masonry, MEP systems all need eyes. Assume full gut rehab until proven otherwise.

02
Live Intelligence
Regulatory Scout

Here's the bombshell. The Site Scout assumed the building is a contributing resource in the historic district. The Regulatory Scout found that every single neighbor on this block is coded non-contributing in the National Register inventory. 1244, 1247, 1252, 1258, 1262 S Shelby. All NC. The surveyors flagged this entire stretch as having lost integrity through alterations. 1271 is almost certainly non-contributing too. If that holds, the entire HTC stack collapses.

MK’s correction (May 10, 2026): Adjacent non-contributing status does not automatically disqualify 1271. Each building in the inventory is evaluated on its own integrity. The NC coding of neighboring properties reflects their specific alterations, not a block-wide disqualification. 1271 must receive its own direct evaluation from KHC. The HTC stack remains live until that determination comes back.

But there's a documented appeal pathway, and it has been used successfully on this exact corridor. Allison Cleaners at 1133 S. Shelby was denied contributing status in February 2024. The owner removed a non-historic brick veneer and Mansard roof, revealed the original facade underneath, and won the appeal in June 2024. NPS reversed the determination. That is the playbook for 1271.

The other correction: Kentucky's commercial historic credit is 20%, not 30%. The 30% is owner-occupied residential only, capped at $120K. Combined federal plus state on a commercial project is 40%, not the 50% we first assumed. At $1.5M of qualified rehab expenditures, that's still roughly $490K in equity. But the $150K difference matters.

MK is preparing the KHC email to Katie Wilborn as of May 10, 2026. The corrected pro forma (Market Scenario) is delivered. Next up: run the HTC scenario with the 20/20 stack and model the 1267 lot density play.

04 — The Proof

Built Evidence

These aren't aspirational mood boards. These are real projects, in this market, that either prove the model works or show exactly where it breaks. Every precedent is argued, not just listed.

03
Live Intelligence
Precedent Scout

The case for 1271 Shelby rests on five demonstrable precedents in the Louisville market. The Bellwether proves a sub-25 key boutique hotel works on a Louisville historic corridor. Bakery Square proves the scale and acquisition price are within reach for an individual local developer. The Bardstown Motor Lodge proves the Kentucky HTC capital stack closes with a known equity provider. The Myriad proves the institutional knowledge to execute this project type already lives inside Eric's firm. And Logan Street Market, three blocks away, proves Shelby Park already pulls the pedestrian traffic.

But here's the honest part: The Silver Dollar closed November 2025 after 14 years. The firehouse-to-honky-tonk model ran a full cycle. The lesson isn't that old buildings don't work. The lesson is that single-concept, single-revenue-stream hospitality in a 135-year-old building is fragile. When the founder energy fades, a one-trick concept can't pivot. 1271 should not be a single-concept bar. The model that lasts is layered.

05 — The Terrain

Political Landscape

The councilperson is a Democratic Socialist. The neighborhood association president is a social worker who voted against the last big development on this block. The dominant impact-investment force operates out of an evangelical worldview that progressive locals view as gentrification with a paint job. Done right, this project lands. Done wrong, it becomes a cautionary tale.

04
Live Intelligence
Political Scout

The most important person this team hasn't met yet is Maria Gurren, President of the Shelby Park Neighborhood Association. Not the Mayor. Not the Planning Commission. The neighborhood association president, in the room, before any public announcement. The Louisville Vegan Jerky Co. precedent makes the case: SPNA voted against a project two blocks south of 1271 because the developer hadn't shown up to the association first.

Councilperson JP Lyninger (District 6, Democratic Socialist) is not reflexively anti-development. The pattern in his record is consistency on one question: does this project use public money to enrich private developers, and does it cost residents their housing? A privately-financed historic rehab using federal/state tax credits that doesn't displace any residents sits in a more favorable space than most. The hardest question he'll ask is sincere, not hostile: what does this do for the working-class residents of Shelby Park, beyond preserving a building?

The project will be measured against the Sojourn Church / Access Ventures pattern whether we like it or not. Bryce Butler's impact-investment portfolio is the dominant force in Shelby Park, and progressive locals view it through a gentrification lens. Don't approach Butler in the first 90 days. Establish the project's own identity first. Make the early calls to SPNA, to Lyninger, to Marianne Zickuhr at Preservation Louisville three blocks away.

08 — Intel Feed Protocol

How to Feed the Brain

Every piece of intelligence enters the system the same way. Self-addressed email. Subject line tells the system where it goes. Body carries the payload. One email per finding. Don't bundle.

Email Naming Convention

Send to your own inbox (and cc Eric). Subject line follows this format:

Liquid Dark — [Scout or Topic] — [What This Captures]

Examples:

Liquid Dark — Site — Flood zone confirmation from LOJIC Liquid Dark — Political — Conversation with Maria Gurren notes Liquid Dark — Feasibility — Corrected HTC stack from Katie Wilborn call Liquid Dark — Precedent — Walked the Bellwether, here's what I noticed
Subject line is the filing system. Be specific. Future-you is searching for this.
Body carries what moved, what was decided, and what's next.
Attachments welcome. Screenshots, PDFs, spreadsheet exports.
One email per intel item. Don't bundle unrelated findings.
